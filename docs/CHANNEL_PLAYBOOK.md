# Proof of Origin — Channel Playbook

## Channel promise

We test digital trust claims in public.

Not:
> "Here is a feature in my software."

But:
> "Here is a digital object. I changed it. Shared it. Converted it. Photographed it. Now what evidence survives?"

## Content pillars

### 1. Real or Fake?
Official-looking documents, screenshots, images and AI media.

### 2. HPS Experiments
Controlled tests of exact identity, derivatives and material change.

### 3. Tamper Tests
Numbers, dates, signatures, pages, screenshots and conversions.

### 4. Digital Trust Explained
Hashes, signatures, provenance, C2PA, metadata, OCR and chain of custody.

### 5. AI & Authenticity
AI-generated text, images, video and research content.

### 6. Research Integrity
Human/AI contribution, attribution and provenance for scientific work.

## Title standard

Good:
- I Changed One Number in a Registered Document. Could HPS Detect It?
- I Sent a Signed PDF Through WhatsApp. What Survived?
- Can a Screenshot Still Prove Where It Came From?
- This Document Looks Official. That Is Not Enough.

Weak:
- HPS 1.5 Feature Demo
- Understanding Provenance
- How HPS Works Part 7

## Hook standard

Within the first 12 seconds:

1. show the object;
2. show the controlled change;
3. state the question.

Do not begin with a logo animation or a long personal introduction.

## Experiment standard

A strong experiment has:

- one clearly controlled variable;
- a known original;
- an observable verification result;
- a meaningful interpretation;
- a limitation;
- one obvious next test.

## Trust standard

Never hide a failed test.

If HPS is inconclusive, say so and explain what the failure revealed. Those videos can be more interesting than flawless demos because viewers see the system being stress-tested rather than advertised.
